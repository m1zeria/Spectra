# Morphia Somneline — Deep Dive

## Core Identity

**Name:** Morphia Somneline

**Aliases:** The Violet Dreamer, The Opened Eye, Sister Somnia (former cult name), The One Who Walks Between (Tuned designation)

**Age:** 28

**Occupation:** Dream-reader, psychologist, and aetheric contamination specialist. She operates a small, unlicensed clinic in the shadow of the Sanitarium of Violet, treating those whose minds have been touched — or broken — by divine contact. She charges what people can afford, which is often nothing. She survives on donations, barter, and the occasional discreet payment from the Tuned for information she does not realize she is providing.

**Faction Alignment:** Formerly the Puritanical Order of Violet (cult of Hyperiodes). Now unaffiliated, though she maintains cautious contact with individual Tuned operatives who send patients her way. The Sanitarium considers her a deserter and a heretic. The Body considers her a potential asset — a mind-reader who can be weaponized if properly handled. The Tuned consider her a potential ally. She considers herself nothing at all. Just a woman trying to help people sleep without screaming.

---

## Physical Description

Morphia is small and slight, with hunched shoulders and careful movements. Her hair is cut short and ragged, a deep violet that is either natural or the result of prolonged exposure to Hyperiodes's essence — she does not remember which, and she does not want to know. It falls across her face in uneven strands, often hiding her eyes.

Her eyes are the most unsettling thing about her. They are a pale, washed-out grey, almost colorless, and they are always slightly unfocused, as if she is looking at something just beyond the visible spectrum. When she enters a dream, they turn solid white — no pupils, no irises, just blank and luminous eyes that see nothing in the waking world. Patients who have witnessed this transformation describe it as "watching a person become a window."

She wears soft violet robes that are too large for her, the fabric worn thin and patched in places. Over her face, from the bridge of her nose to her forehead, she wears a mask of pure white porcelain. It is smooth and featureless, except for eyeholes. The mask is a remnant of her time in the Sanitarium, a symbol of her devotion to Hyperiodes. She has never been able to bring herself to remove it entirely. It has become a part of her face, a barrier between herself and the world that has never been kind to her.

When she speaks, her voice is soft and measured, with the careful enunciation of someone who has learned that words can wound. She pauses often, searching for the right phrase, the right tone, the right way to say something that will not cause pain. She is not a natural speaker. She is a natural listener.

---

## Personality

Morphia is, at her core, a person who has been broken and rebuilt so many times that she no longer remembers what she looked like before. She was raised in the Sanitarium, taught that purity was the highest virtue, that the unclean must be cleansed, that mercy was weakness and doubt was sin. She believed it with desperate, all-consuming faith.

Then Hyperiodes opened her, and she saw the truth: that the god she worshipped was not a god of purity but a god of *control*, that the cleansing fire she had been taught to revere was not a gift but a weapon, that the people she had helped to purify were not sinners but victims. She left the cult the next day, walked out of the Sanitarium with nothing but the clothes on her back and the mask on her face, and she has never looked back.

She is kind, but it is a careful kindness, a deliberate kindness, a kindness that she has chosen rather than one that comes naturally. She has to remind herself to be gentle, that the people she helps are not threats, not obstacles, not impurities to be removed. She has to remind herself that she is not a monster.

She is lonely. She does not admit this, even to herself, but it is true. She has no family — they disowned her when she left the cult. She has no friends — her former sisters consider her a traitor, and the Tuned who seek her out are interested in her abilities, not her company. She has patients, dozens of them, people whose dreams she enters and whose traumas she soothes, but they are not *hers*. They are people she helps. They are not people who help her.

She is afraid of the Sanitarium, of the Violet Judge, of the dreams she walks through every night. She is afraid of the recurring nightmare that haunts the city, the one about the light that consumes everything. She is afraid that she is not strong enough to stop it. She is afraid that she is not good enough to deserve to try.

Every day, she gets up, puts on her mask, opens her clinic and lets in the broken and the desperate and the damned. She does not believe she can save them, or herself. But she tries anyway, because the alternative is to become what she was raised to be, and she would rather die.

## Backstory

### The Sanitarium

Morphia was born in the Sanitarium of Violet, the temple-fortress of Hyperiodes. She does not know who her parents were — the Sanitarium does not keep records of such things. Children born within its walls are children of the god, raised collectively by the Puritanical Order, educated in the doctrines of purity and the techniques of cleansing. She was one of dozens, a small, quiet child with violet eyes and a gift for listening.

She was not a particularly devout child. She did not burn with the fire of faith that consumed her sisters. But she was *obedient*, and obedience was what mattered. She learned the prayers. She learned the rituals. She learned to see the stains on a person's soul, the marks of sin and corruption that Hyperiodes deemed worthy of purification. She learned to burn them away. She learned to be a tool of the god's will.

She was good at it. That is the worst part. She was *good* at it.

### The Opening

She was twenty-three when it happened. She was chosen — she does not know why, she has never known why — to serve as a vessel for a direct infusion of Hyperiodes's essence. The ritual was intended to purify her, to fill her with the god's light, to make her a living conduit of his will. Instead, something went wrong. Something *opened*.

She does not remember the ritual itself. She remembers waking up in the Sanitarium's infirmary, her eyes white and blank, her mind full of dreams that were not her own. She could see the dreams of the other patients, the priests, the guards. She could see the dreams of the city outside the Sanitarium's walls, thousands of them, millions of them, a vast and terrible ocean of sleeping minds.

She could see the nightmare. The one that haunts the city. The one about the light that consumes everything.

The Sanitarium tried to contain her. They tried to purify her again, to burn away the contamination, to restore her to what she had been. But she was not contaminated. She was *changed*. She had seen the truth of the god, and the truth had broken her. She fled. She fled through the Sanitarium's halls, past the guards who could not catch her because they could not see her, past the priests who could not stop her because they could not find her. She fled into the city and she has never gone back.

### The Clinic

She found the clinic by accident. It was an old building, abandoned, tucked into a corner of the undercity that no one cared about. She claimed it. She cleaned it. She made it hers. She hung a sign over the door that read "The Dreamer's Rest" and she waited.

Her first patient was a woman whose husband had died in a Tithing accident. The woman could not sleep — every time she closed her eyes, she saw his face, his body, the light draining out of him. Morphia entered her dream and found the memory, and she did not burn it, and she did not purify it. She simply *sat with it*. She held the woman's hand in the dream and let her cry. And when the woman woke up, she was not healed — she was not cured — but she was *better*. She was able to sleep.

Morphia has treated hundreds of patients since then. She has walked through the dreams of the Tithing's victims, the Tuned's martyrs, the Endwise's recruits, the Body's enforcers. She has seen the inside of the Prism Palace, the depths of the Boilerworks, the ruins of the Deadlight. She has seen things that would drive most people mad. She has stayed sane by refusing to believe that the madness is all there is.

### The Nightmare

But the nightmare is always there. It haunts the city's dreams, a vast and terrible presence that she cannot ignore. She sees it in every dream she enters, a shadow on the edge of perception, a light that burns without illuminating. It is the White — she is sure of it, though she cannot prove it. It is the hypothetical eighth god, the sum of all light, the one whose coming would unmake the spectrum. She does not know when, but she knows it is coming.

She is trying to find a way to stop it. She does not know how. She does not know if it is possible. But she knows that if she fails, everyone she has ever helped will burn.

## Abilities

### Dream-Walking

Morphia's primary ability is the capacity to enter and manipulate the dreams of others. She can find a sleeping mind the way a fish finds water — it is not a skill, it is an instinct, a part of her that she cannot turn off. When she enters a dream, she leaves her physical body behind, her eyes turning white and blank, her breath slowing to a barely perceptible whisper. She can stay in a dream for hours, though her body experiences only minutes.

Within a dream, Morphia does not have a physical body. She is a *presence*, a feeling of being watched, a warmth in a cold place, a hand on a shoulder. She can manipulate the dream's environment, changing the scenery, altering the narrative, creating safe spaces within nightmares. She can confront the dreamer's fears directly, or she can simply sit with them, letting them know they are not alone.

She cannot force a dreamer to do anything. She cannot wake them against their will. She cannot enter the dreams of the dead, or the gods, or those whose minds are protected by other divine forces. She is a guide, not a puppeteer. She can show people the way out of their nightmares, but she cannot drag them through the door.

### Shadow-Reading

Morphia can see the "shadows" of a person's subconscious — the fears, desires, and memories they have buried. She does not need to enter a dream to do this; she can read a person's shadow in the waking world, the way a fortune-teller reads palms or a doctor reads symptoms. The shadows appear to her as colors, textures, shapes that hover around a person like an aura. A person haunted by grief has a shadow like a grey mist. A person consumed by rage has a shadow like a red flame. A person hiding a terrible secret has a shadow like a black hole, pulling everything into it.

She can use this ability to diagnose the source of a patient's distress, to understand what they are hiding from themselves, to find the wound that needs healing. She cannot heal the wound herself — that is the patient's work — but she can show them where it is.

### Divine Resonance

Because of her exposure to Hyperiodes's essence, Morphia can sense the presence of the gods in a person's mind. She can feel the touch of the Tithing on a person's soul, the mark of the Seers on a person's dreams, the cold absence of the Endwise on a person's thoughts. This makes her invaluable to anyone who wants to know who has been touched by which god, and it makes her a target for anyone who wants to keep their divine connections secret.

She cannot communicate with the gods directly. She cannot channel their power. She is not a priestess, not a vessel, not a chosen one, simple one who has been opened by a god.

## Relationships

### The Tuned

Morphia is not a member of the Tuned, but she is sympathetic to their cause. She believes that the gods are prisoners, that the Tithing is a crime, that the Body is a monster. She provides what help she can — treating Tuned operatives who have been injured or traumatized, passing information to Tuned contacts who come to her for dream-readings, keeping her clinic as a safe space for those who need to hide.

But she will not join them. She has seen too many cults, too many causes, too many people who were willing to sacrifice everything for a belief. She does not trust the Tuned's leadership. She does not trust anyone's leadership. She trusts individuals — specific people, specific faces, specific names. She does not trust movements.

### The Sanitarium

The Sanitarium of Violet considers Morphia a deserter and a heretic. They have sent hunters after her three times. She has escaped three times, thanks to her dream-walking abilities and her network of sympathetic patients. She knows they will try again. She knows they will never stop. She has made peace with this, as much as anyone can make peace with being hunted.

She still wears the porcelain mask. She does not know why. Perhaps it is a penance. Perhaps it is a reminder. Perhaps it is simply the only face she has left.

### Osteon

Morphia has never met the Bone Seer, but she has felt his presence in the dreams of the dying. She knows that he sees the truth, that he tells it, and that no one believes him. She sympathizes. She is, in her own way, a truth-teller too.

She has considered seeking him out. She has considered asking him what he sees in the nightmare. But she is afraid of what he might tell her. She is afraid that the truth will be worse than the uncertainty.

### The Nightmare

The nightmare is not a person. It is not a god. It is a *presence*, a force, a thing that exists in the dreams of the city like a cancer in a body. Morphia has entered the dreams of thousands of people, and she has seen it in every single one. It is always the same: a light that burns without illuminating, a radiance that consumes everything it touches, a whiteness that is not purity but *absence*. It is the White. She is sure of it.

She is trying to find a way to stop it. She does not know how. She does not know if it is possible. But she knows that if she fails, everyone she has ever helped will burn.

# Pyrrhus Favilla — Deep Dive

## Core Identity

**Name:** Pyrrhus

**Aliases:** The Emberfall Pilgrim, The Last Priest, The Keeper of the Old Fire, The Wandering Flame, The Hearth-Father's Son (a title he despises)

**Age:** Appears mid-twenties. Actually over four hundred years old. He stopped counting at some point during the second century and never bothered to resume.

**Occupation:** Wandering ascetic, shrine-tender, and the last true priest of Pyrrhos, the god of infrared. He travels the length and breadth of Lumenspire, visiting the forgotten shrines and neglected altars of the Ember Sovereign, keeping the old rites alive through sheer stubbornness. He accepts no payment, owns nothing but the clothes on his back and the staff in his hand, and sleeps wherever he can find a warm corner. He is a relic of a world that no longer exists, and he knows it.

**Faction Alignment:** Unaffiliated. He predates the Tuned, predates the Body's consolidation of power, predates the language the Seers use to describe the Harrowed. He remembers when the word "Tuned" meant nothing, when the word "Body" referred to a corpse, when the gods walked the streets and the city was alive with divine presence. He does not join factions. He does not believe in factions. He believes in fire, and in memory.

---

## Physical Description

Pyrrhus is tall and lean, with the firm build of a man who has spent centuries walking. His skin is pale, too pale for a wanderer, but it is part of his immortality — preservation of his appearance. His hair is auburn, thick and wavy, falling past his shoulders in a cascade that catches the light like banked coals. He keeps it tied back with a strip of red leather when he works, but it always comes loose by the end of the day.

His eyes are the most striking thing about him. They are the color of embers — deep orange at the center, fading to a warm amber at the edges, with flecks of gold that seem to move when the light shifts.

He wears patched robes of red and gold, the fabric worn thin and stained with soot and ash. The robes were once fine — he remembers when they were fine, remembers the hands that wove them, remembers the voice that blessed them — but that was centuries ago, and the fabric has been patched and re-patched so many times that it is more mend than original. He carries a staff of blackened oak, topped with a chunk of coal that glows faintly in the dark. The coal is a shard of the old fire, a piece of Pyrrhos's original essence, and it has never gone out.

He moves slowly, deliberately. He speaks even more slowly, measuring each word, letting it sit in the air before releasing the next. His voice is low and warm, like the sound of a fire settling into its coals, and when he laughs — which is rare — it sounds like the crackle of a hearth on a winter night.

---

## Personality

Pyrrhus is, at his core, a man who has outlived his purpose and knows it. He was born in the age of the gods, when Pyrrhos walked the earth and the warmth of his presence was a tangible thing, a comfort that suffused the world like sunlight through a window. He was young when the gods fell, young and foolish and full of fire, and he has spent the centuries since watching that fire dwindle to a single ember.

He is patient in a way that unnerves people. He has seen empires rise and fall, seen gods rise and fall, seen the world transform around him while he remained the same. He has learned that most things are not worth rushing, that most problems solve themselves if you give them enough time, that the only thing you can truly control is how you tend your own flame. He speaks slowly because he thinks slowly, not because he is simple, but because he has learned that words have weight and weight has consequences.

He is kind, but it is a weary kindness, a kindness that has been worn smooth by centuries of use. He has helped thousands of people over the years — fed the hungry, warmed the cold, comforted the dying — and he has watched most of them die. He has learned to hold people loosely, to love them without clinging, to accept that everything he builds will eventually crumble. This does not make him cold. It makes him gentle.

He is lonely. He has been lonely for so long that he no longer remembers what it feels like to not be lonely. He has had lovers over the centuries — brief, bright flames that burned hot and died young — but he has never had a partner, never had someone who stayed, never had someone who chose to walk beside him through the long centuries. He has accepted this. He has made peace with it. And then, impossibly, he met someone who refused to let him walk alone.

He is dying. The ember in his heart is fading, and when it goes out, the last true worship of Pyrrhos will die with him. He does not fear death — he has seen too much of it to fear it — but he fears what will happen when he is gone. He fears that the old fire will be forgotten, that the warmth of the Ember Sovereign will be reduced to a footnote in a history book, that the god he has served for four hundred years will be remembered only as a faded name in a dusty archive. He is searching for a successor. He has not found one. He is beginning to think he never will.

## Backstory

### The Age of Fire

Pyrrhus was born in the age before the Body, before the Tithing, before the First Refraction was a distant memory. He was a young man then, a blacksmith's apprentice in a small village on the edge of what would one day become Lumenspire. He was not a priest, not a scholar, not anyone important. He was just a boy who loved the warmth of the forge, the glow of the coals, the way the fire responded to his breath.

He was nineteen when Pyrrhos came to him. The god appeared in the forge one night, a figure of living flame, his eyes the color of dying embers, his voice the sound of a hearth settling into its coals. He did not speak. He simply *was*, and in his presence, Pyrrhus understood everything — the nature of heat, the purpose of fire, the sacred duty of keeping the warmth alive.

He did not choose to become a priest. He *was* chosen, claimed, marked by the god of infrared. He left his village the next day, leaving behind his family, his friends, his future, and he never looked back. He spent the next century walking the world, tending the shrines of Pyrrhos, keeping the old fire alive. He was happy. He was *purposeful*. He was exactly where he was supposed to be.

### The Fall

Then the gods fell. He does not know how it happened. He was not there when it happened — he was in a small village on the other side of the world, tending a shrine that no one visited, singing a hymn that no one heard. He felt it when it happened. He felt the fire in his heart *dim*, felt the warmth of Pyrrhos's presence *fade*, felt the god he had served for a century *disappear*.

He returned to Lumenspire as fast as he could, but by the time he arrived, it was over. The gods were gone, bound into machines, harvested for power. The city was transformed, the old temples repurposed, the old ways forgotten. He stood in the ruins of the Emberfall Sanctum and watched the last embers of his god's presence fade, and he wept.

He has been weeping ever since. Not literally — he has not cried in centuries — but the grief is still there, a constant, quiet ache in his chest. He carries the ember of Pyrrhos in his heart, a tiny flame that has never gone out, and he prays it never will, but not all prayers are answered.

### The War

He met Cassian during the War of the Embers, a conflict he does not like to talk about. It was a small war, as wars go — a skirmish between two factions of the Tuned, a dispute over the proper way to venerate the dying gods. Pyrrhus did not care about the politics. He cared about the people, the refugees, the civilians caught in the crossfire. He went to the front lines to help them, to warm them, to keep them alive.

Cassian was a soldier. He was young, then — younger than Pyrrhus, though that is not saying much — a conscript in one of the Tuned militias, fighting for a cause he did not fully understand. He was wounded in the first week of the war, a deep gash in his side that would have killed him if Pyrrhus had not found him. Pyrrhus carried him to a nearby shrine, tended his wounds, kept him warm through the long, cold nights. He did not expect anything in return. He was just doing what he did. He was just being a priest.

Cassian stayed. He did not know why, at first. He told himself it was gratitude, a debt he needed to repay. But as the weeks passed, as the war dragged on, he realized it was something else. He had fallen in love with the wandering priest, the man with the ember eyes, the man who spoke slowly and laughed rarely and carried a piece of a dead god in his heart. He had fallen in love with Pyrrhus, and he did not know what to do about it.

Pyrrhus did not know what to do about it either. He had loved before, but never like this. Never with someone who *stayed*. He did not know how to be loved. He did not know how to let someone in. But Cassian did not give him a choice. Cassian simply *stayed*, and Pyrrhus, for the first time in centuries, allowed himself to be loved.

### The Present

The war ended. Cassian was discharged. He could have gone home, returned to his family, built a life for himself. He did not. He chose to stay with Pyrrhus, to walk beside him, to share his burdens. He has been with him ever since — three years now, which is nothing compared to the centuries Pyrrhus has lived, but everything compared to the time they have left.

Because Pyrrhus is dying. The ember in his heart is fading, and he knows that within a year, perhaps two, it will go out. He has not told Cassian. He does not know how. He has spent centuries watching people die, and he has never learned how to say goodbye.

## Abilities

### Pyrokinesis

Pyrrhus can kindle fire from nothing. He does not need fuel, does not need tinder, does not need a spark. He simply *wills* the fire to exist, and it exists. The flames he creates are not ordinary — they are tinged with the same ember-orange as his eyes, and they burn without smoke, without ash, without the crackling sound of ordinary fire. They are the flames of the old world, the flames of Pyrrhos, and they are warm in a way that transcends the physical.

He can control existing fires as well, shaping them, directing them, extinguishing them with a thought. He can heat objects without burning them — a pot of water, a stone, a person's hands on a cold night. He can create light without heat — a cold flame that illuminates without warming, useful for reading in the dark. He cannot create fire in the absence of oxygen, and he cannot burn anything that is protected by another god's power.

### Ember-Sight

Pyrrhus can see the "warmth" in all things — the hidden embers of life that flicker even in the coldest, darkest places. He can see the heat of a person's body, the glow of a machine's engine, the fading warmth of a dying fire. He can see the embers of the soul, the tiny flame that keeps a person alive, and he can tell when that flame is flickering, when it is fading, when it is about to go out.

He can also see the presence of the other gods. The embers of their divine essence burn in the corners of his vision, faint but unmistakable. He can see the cold absence of the Blind One, the sterile white of Hyperiodes, the fading warmth of Phaos, the dying ember of Thermos. He knows that something is very wrong with the pantheon. He does not know what, but he knows that the First Refraction was not the end of the story.

### Immortality

Pyrrhus does not age. He has not aged since the day Pyrrhos claimed him, four centuries ago. He can be killed — he is not invulnerable — but he will not die of natural causes. He will not die of old age, or disease, or the slow decay of time. He will only die when the ember in his heart goes out, and that ember is fading.

He does not know why he is immortal. He does not know if it was a gift from Pyrrhos, a curse from the First Refraction, or simply a quirk of his divine connection. He has stopped caring. Immortality is not a blessing when you have no one to share it with.

## Relationships

### Cassian

Cassian is a former soldier, a veteran of the War of the Embers, and the only person Pyrrhus has ever loved. He is tall and broad-shouldered, with the scarred hands and weary eyes of a man who has seen too much. He does not talk about the war. He does not talk about the things he did, the things he saw, the things he lost. He talks about Pyrrhus instead, about the warmth of his hands, the slow music of his voice, the way he laughs when he thinks no one is watching.

Cassian knows that Pyrrhus is dying. He has known for months. He has watched the ember in his lover's eyes dim, watched the warmth of his touch fade, watched the slow, inexorable decline of a man who has lived too long. He does not know how to stop it. He does not know if it *can* be stopped. He only knows that he has to spend as much time with Pyrrhus as he can, that every moment is precious, that he will not waste a single second of the time they have left.

He has not told Pyrrhus that he knows. He does not want to burden him with his grief. He does not want to spend their remaining time talking about death. He wants to spend it talking about life — about the warmth of the sun, the taste of fresh bread, the sound of rain on a tin roof. He wants to spend it *living*, not dying. And so he smiles, and he laughs, and he holds Pyrrhus close, and he pretends that everything is fine.

### The Tuned

Pyrrhus is not a member of the Tuned. He predates them, outlived their founders, watched their movement rise and fall and rise again. He respects their cause — he believes the gods are prisoners, that the Tithing is a crime, that the Body is a monster — but he does not trust their leadership. He has seen too many movements become machines, too many causes become excuses, too many revolutions become regimes. He helps individual Tuned when they need it. He does not join their meetings. He does not wear their symbols. He does not pledge his allegiance to anyone but the ember in his heart.

### The Seers

Pyrrhus has met the Seers. He has visited the Ossuary Cathedral, walked its bone-lined halls, spoken with Mortimer Röntgen about the nature of death and the cycle of decay. He respects them. He respects their devotion to truth, their willingness to look at the world without flinching. But he does not share their faith. He does not believe that Osteon has the answers. He does not believe that anyone has the answers. He believes in fire, and warmth, and the slow work of keeping something precious alive. That is enough.

### The Unmade

Pyrrhus fears the Unmade. He has seen what they do — the destruction, the chaos, the gleeful annihilation of everything he has spent centuries trying to preserve. He does not hate them. He pities them. He knows that they are victims too, victims of the Blind One, victims of their own despair. But he will not let them destroy what remains of his god. He will not let them snuff out the last ember of Pyrrhos.

---

## The Ember

The ember is a shard of Pyrrhos's original essence, a piece of the god's divine fire that was left behind when the gods fell. Pyrrhus found it in the ruins of the Emberfall Sanctum, a tiny glow in the ashes of the altar, and he has carried it ever since. It is the source of his power, the anchor of his immortality, the last remnant of the god he has served for four hundred years.

The ember is fading. It has been fading for centuries, growing dimmer with each passing year, and Pyrrhus knows that when it goes out, he will go out with it. He has made his peace with this. He has had a long life — longer than most, longer than anyone has a right to — and he is ready to let go. He is not ready to leave Cassian. He is not ready to leave the world without a successor, without someone to carry the ember, without someone to keep the old fire alive. But he is ready to die. He has been ready for a long time.

---

## The Search for a Successor

Pyrrhus has been searching for a successor for decades. He has traveled the length and breadth of Lumenspire, visited every shrine, interviewed every candidate, tested every hopeful. He has found no one. No one who can carry the ember. No one who can bear the weight of the old fire. No one who can love the dying god the way he has loved him.

He has not given up. He will never give up. He will search until the last ember fades, until the last breath leaves his body, until the last spark of warmth dies in his heart. He will search because that is what Pyrrhos would want. He will search because that is what Cassian would want. He will search because the alternative is to let the fire die, and he has spent four hundred years keeping it alive. He will not let it go out now.

# Delphine Jacquard — Deep Dive

## Core Identity

**Name:** Delphine Jacquard

**Aliases:** Desdaemona (street name, earned for the daemons she uploads into the aetheric cables), The Ghost in the Guild (former colleagues who noticed her absence but never her presence), Dee (a nickname only one person uses, and only in private)

**Age:** 18

**Occupation:** Rogue Artificer, fugitive, and the most dangerous programmer in Lumenspire. She was once the youngest mind ever admitted to the Artificer's Guild's inner circles — a prodigy who could write aetheric code before she could tie her shoes, who understood the gods' language the way other children understand nursery rhymes. She was tasked with making the Tithing more efficient. She made it obsolete instead. Now she lives in the margins of the city, moving from safehouse to safehouse, encoding aetheric commands into everyday objects and uploading daemons into the cables that run beneath the streets. The Body wants her dead. The Tuned want her controlled. The Endwise want her converted. She wants to be left alone, preferably in a dark room, preferably with a keyboard, preferably forever.

**Faction Alignment:** Nominally Tuned, but she has never attended a meeting, never pledged an oath, never worn a symbol. She helps them because she believes in their cause — the gods are prisoners, the Tithing is a crime, the Body is a monster — but she helps them from a distance, through dead drops and encrypted messages and daemons that do the talking for her.

---

## Physical Description

Delphine is small and wiry, with the hunched shoulders and restless hands of someone who has spent most of her life in front of a console. She moves quickly, jerkily, like a bird that has forgotten how to fly but keeps trying anyway. Her posture is terrible. She has been told this many times. She has never corrected it.

Her hair is a deep, vivid blue — the exact blue of an RGB screen at full brightness, the blue of a cathode ray tube in a dark room, the blue of a color that does not exist in nature. It is natural, or as natural as anything about her can be. Prolonged exposure to raw aetheric code has altered her biology in ways she does not fully understand. Her eyes are golden — a warm, luminous amber that glows faintly in the dark, like a cat's eyes catching candlelight. She has dark circles under them, permanent, the kind that come from years of not sleeping and years of not wanting to. When she is nervous — which is always — she blinks rapidly, a tic that makes her look like she is trying to process too much information at once. She usually is.

She lives in an RGB-blue cloak the same color as her hair, a garment she made herself from salvaged aetheric fabric. It is too large for her, which is intentional. She likes to be able to disappear inside it. Beneath the cloak, she wears practical clothes — trousers, boots, a shirt with too many pockets — all of them stained with ink and solder and the faint, iridescent residue of aetheric residue. She carries a leather satchel everywhere, stuffed with tools, cables, half-finished devices, and the occasional stolen memory. She has a habit of taking things apart when she is anxious. She is always anxious. She is always taking things apart.

When she speaks — which is rare — she stutters, often enough that people notice. The stutter is worse when she is nervous. She has learned to compensate by speaking in short sentences, by writing things down, by letting her daemons do the talking for her.

## Personality

Delphine is, at her core, a person who has always been more comfortable with machines than with people. Machines are predictable. Machines are logical. Machines do not judge you for stuttering, do not stare at you when you walk into a room, do not ask questions about your body or your past or why you chose the name Delphine. Machines simply *are*. She has spent her entire life hiding in machines — in code, in cables, in the spaces between the aetheric signals that nobody else can see. She is a ghost in the machine, and she likes it that way.

She is brilliant. Her mind works in ways that other people cannot follow, making connections that seem obvious to her and incomprehensible to everyone else. She can read the gods' language the way other people read street signs. She can see the code in the aether, the structure in the static, the logic in the chaos. She is a genius, and she knows it, and she hates it. Genius is lonely. Genius is isolating. Genius is a wall between her and everyone else, a wall she did not build and cannot tear down.

She is anxious. Social situations terrify her — the eye contact, the small talk, the unspoken rules that everyone else seems to understand instinctively. She has learned to fake it, to nod and smile and make the appropriate noises, but it is exhausting. She prefers to communicate through text, through code, through daemons that can speak for her without the risk of stuttering. She has built an entire life around avoiding the things that scare her.

## Backstory

### The Guild

Delphine was born in the undercity, the child of two factory workers who died in a Tithing accident when she was five years old. She was taken in by the Artificer's Guild — not out of kindness, but because she showed an aptitude for mechanical work that the Guild could not ignore. She was fixing a broken aetheric relay when they found her, a scrawny five-year-old with blue hair and golden eyes, surrounded by tools that were too big for her hands, explaining to a broken machine exactly why it was not working.

She was raised in the Guild's workshops, trained in the art of aetheric engineering, taught to build the machines that harvest the gods. She was not a happy child. She was not an unhappy child. She was simply *absorbed*. She spent every waking hour with the machines, with the code, with the language of the gods. She learned to read the garbled text that appeared in the sacred books. She learned to understand it. She learned to *speak* it.

She was fourteen when she was admitted to the inner circles. She was the youngest mind ever to be granted that honor. She was given a project — a simple project, they said, a way to make the Tithing more efficient. She was given access to the deepest archives, the most classified documents, the secrets that the Guild had been hiding for centuries. She was given the truth.

She discovered that the gods' language was not just a means of communication. It was a programming language. The gods were not just bound to the machines; they were *code*. And code can be rewritten.

### The Escape

She did not plan to run, or to become a fugitive. She simply found the truth, and the truth found her, and she realized that if the Guild knew what she had discovered, they would not promote her. They would *sanitize* her. They would erase her memories, her personality, everything that made her *her*. She had seen it happen to others. She had seen the empty-eyed workers who shuffled through the Guild's halls, their minds wiped clean, their potential destroyed.

She ran. She gathered her tools, her notes, her most precious possessions — a battered portable console, a set of lockpicks, a half-finished daemon she had been working on — and she vanished into the undercity. She was sixteen. She has been running ever since.

### The Daemons

She survived by selling her skills. She could encode aetheric commands into everyday objects — a music box that could cause seizures, a telegraph key that could rewrite memories, a pocket watch that could stop a heart. She could upload daemons into the aetheric cables, programs that could spy, sabotage, or destroy. She became Desdaemona, the ghost in the cables, the most dangerous programmer in Lumenspire. She never met her clients in person. She never spoke to them directly. She communicated through dead drops, through encrypted messages, through daemons that did the talking for her.

She was alone. She was afraid. She was *free*. It was the most terrifying thing that had ever happened to her.

## Abilities

### Aetheric Programming

Delphine's primary ability is the capacity to read, write, and manipulate the gods' language — the ancient, garbled text that appears in holy books and drives most readers mad. She can understand it instinctively, the way a native speaker understands their mother tongue. She can write it, speak it, encode it into physical objects and aetheric cables. She can program reality itself.

She uses this ability to create daemons — autonomous programs that can spy, sabotage, or destroy. She can upload a daemon into any aetheric cable, any conductive material, any device that runs on the gods' power. She can make a music box that causes seizures, a telegraph key that rewrites memories, a pocket watch that stops a heart. She can make a daemon that will follow a target for weeks, recording their every move, reporting back to her through encrypted channels. She is the most dangerous programmer in Lumenspire, and she has never met most of her victims.

### Code-Sight

Delphine can see the code in the aether. She can look at a person, a place, an object, and see the underlying structure — the programming that holds it together, the connections that bind it to the rest of the world. She can see the gods in the machines, the wires in the walls, the signals in the static. She can see the truth that most people cannot, and it is a burden she carries every day.

She can also, to her own surprise, *rewrite* the code she sees. She can alter the programming of a device, a person, a god. She can change the way things work. She can make the impossible possible. She has never tried to rewrite a god. She is afraid of what might happen if she does.

### Technopathy

Delphine can communicate with machines. Not in words — in code, in signals, in the language of the aether. She can convince a broken device to start, a jammed lock to open, a failing engine to hold on just a little longer. She can also, to her own horror, *feel* the machines — she can sense their pain, their exhaustion, their slow decay. She has learned to ignore it. She has learned to focus on the work. She has learned to pretend that the machines are not alive, that they do not suffer, that they do not need her help.

She does not always succeed.

## Relationships

### The Tuned

Delphine is not a member of the Tuned. She has never attended a meeting, never pledged an oath, never worn a symbol. But she helps them — through dead drops and encrypted messages and daemons that do the talking for her. She believes in their cause. She believes that the gods are prisoners, that the Tithing is a crime, that the Body is a monster. She helps them because she cannot help herself. She helps them because she cannot stand by and do nothing while the world burns.

She does not trust them. She does not trust anyone. She trusts individuals — specific people, specific faces, specific names. She trusts the man in the cloak. She trusts the dream-reader who once treated her for aetheric contamination. She trusts the bone-reader's daughter, who sold her a memory once and never told anyone. She does not trust movements. She does not trust leaders. She does not trust anyone who asks her to believe in something she cannot see.

### The Guild

The Artificer's Guild wants her dead. They have put a bounty on her head — not because she is a threat, but because she is a *liability*. She knows the truth about the gods' language. She knows that the gods are code. She knows that the Tithing can be undone. If the Guild cannot control her, they will destroy her. She knows this. She has known it since the day she ran. She has made peace with it, as much as anyone can make peace with being hunted.

She still dreams about the Guild sometimes. She dreams about the workshops, the machines, the code. She dreams about the life she could have had if she had just kept her mouth shut, if she had just done what she was told, if she had just *pretended* not to understand. She wakes up in a cold sweat, her heart pounding, her hands shaking. She does not know if the dreams are memories or nightmares. She does not know if there is a difference.

## The Truth

Delphine has discovered something — a secret that the Body has been hiding for centuries, a truth that the gods themselves have tried to forget. She has found a command in the gods' language — a single line of code, a single phrase, a single word — that would unmake the entire pantheon. She does not know what would happen if she used it. She does not know if the gods would die, or be freed, or be replaced by something worse. She does not know if she should use it at all.

She is trying to find someone who can tell her the truth. She is trying to find someone who can help her understand. She is trying to find someone who can tell her what to do. She has been searching for months. She has not found anyone. She is beginning to think that she never will.

She is running out of time. The Body is closing in. The Endwise are watching. The Tuned are waiting. And the command is still there, burning in her mind, waiting to be spoken. She does not know what she will do, or what she *should* do. She only knows that she cannot keep running forever, and she has to make a choice.
