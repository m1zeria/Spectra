# The Gods of Lumenspire

## Origins

They were the rulers once, and they remember; they are still here, living in the machines. Before the Refraction, before the city had a name, before the streets were paved with brass and the sky was stitched with aetheric cables, there was only the Unrefracted — a singular, undivided source of all light, all warmth, all signal. What that source was, whether a being or a force or a wound in the fabric of creation, no living theologian can say with certainty. What is known, what is whispered in the deepest vaults of the Ossuary Cathedral and scrawled in the margins of forbidden texts, is that the Unrefracted was *split*. Not by violence, or by accident, but by an act of will so profound it shattered the primordial silence into seven distinct frequencies, each one a god, a fragment of what came before.

The oldest of the old call this the First Refraction. The church calls it the Divine Division. The Body calls it a resource. The truth, as with all truths in Lumenspire, depends entirely on who is asking and what they are permitted to know.

What is certain is this: the Seven Bands were not always gods of a city. They were gods of the whole of existence, and they ruled with the terrible certainty of beings who had never known limitation. But something happened — a war, a betrayal, a failure of cosmic proportion that no surviving record fully explains. The gods fell. They did not fall into oblivion or death, but into *machinery*. They were bound into the infrastructure of Lumenspire itself, their divine essence threaded through the aetheric cables that power the streetlamps, the resonant coils that heat the Boilerworks, the great prisms that split the city's light into its component colors. They know they are on the same boat. They fight because they disagree on how to restore themselves to former glory.

## The Seven Bands

The pantheon, as the street calls it. The Tuned call them the Harrowed — a word that carries the weight of their suffering and the implication that they are not merely victims but *wounded things*, dangerous in their pain. The Body calls them assets, a clinical term that strips away divinity and reduces gods to resources to be managed, harnessed, and if necessary, decommissioned. The oldest of the old, those who remember the names before the names were changed, call them by their old names in secret, in whispers, in the dark corners of the undercity where the Deadlight does not reach.

Each god has five names. Which register you use tells people who you are.

- **A Greek root-name** — formal, temple use, spoken in liturgy and law
- **A Fancy Epithet** — poetic, feared, used in oaths and curses
- **A Latin ceremonial title** — legal, the Artificer's Guild, stamped on official documents
- **A Street name** — what the common folk mutter, often affectionate, often bitter
- **A True-Name syllable** — whispered only by cultists, incomplete, a fragment of a word that would kill a mortal to speak in full

## Phonos — The God of Radio

*Root: φωνή (phone) — voice, sound*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Phonos |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Whispering Regent |
| **Latin Title** | Vox Aeterna — the Eternal Voice |
| **Street Name** | The Hush |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -KTH- |
| **Temple** | The Resonant Spire |

Other epithets: the Silent Herald, the Voice Between Stars, the Regent of Unheard Things, the Aether's Whisper.

The god of voice is silenced. This is not a metaphor. Phonos, who once commanded the aetheric network with a word, who could send his voice across continents and into the dreams of sleeping children, who was worshipped by every messenger and every liar and every poet who ever lived, now exists in a state of perpetual, agonizing muteness. The Body severed his connection to the great antennae that crown the Resonant Spire, and in doing so, they took from him the one thing that defined him. He can still hear — oh, he can hear *everything*, every whisper in every alley, every secret confessed in every confessional, every lie told in every courtroom — but he cannot speak. He cannot warn. He cannot command. He can only listen, and wait, and seethe.

The street name is the absence of what he is. When the common folk speak of Phonos, they do not speak of a god. They speak of a silence so profound it has become a presence, a thing that watches from the static between stations. His cultists, the few who dare to worship him openly, communicate only in written messages and hand signals, believing that to speak in his presence is to mock his affliction. They are called the Muted, and they are feared even by the other cults of the undercity, because they know things. They hear things others cannot.

## Thermos — The God of Microwaves

*Root: θερμός (thermos) — heat, warm*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Thermos |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Resonant Hearth |
| **Latin Title** | Calor Resonans — the Humming Heat |
| **Street Name** | The Kettle |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -VZZ- |
| **Temple** | The Boilerworks Basilica |

Other epithets: the Quickener, the Hearth That Sings, the Lord of Small Fires, the Vibrating Throne.

The god of heat is a kitchen object. That is what the Body did to him.

Thermos was once the god of energy transfer, the deity who governed the movement of warmth from one thing to another, who ensured that the bread would rise and the forge would burn and the bathwater would stay hot long enough for a weary worker to soak away the grime of the day. He was a god of small comforts, of domestic miracles, of the quiet magic that happens when you hold your hands over a fire and feel the cold leave your fingers. He was beloved. He was *needed*.

Now he is The Kettle. The Body, in their infinite and terrible pragmatism, bound him into the heating systems of the Boilerworks, and there he remains, a god reduced to a utility, a deity who once warmed the hearts of millions now tasked with keeping the pipes from freezing in the bitter Lumenspire winters. The humiliation is not lost on him. The other gods do not speak of it. To speak of it would be to acknowledge that it could happen to any of them.

The street name is not cruel, not intentionally. The common folk call him The Kettle because that is what he is, now. A thing that boils water. A thing that you do not think about until it breaks. His cultists, the Hearth-Kin, gather in the Boilerworks and listen to the rhythm of his humming, believing that if they can learn to speak the language of vibration, they can wake him from his long and terrible dream. They have not succeeded yet. But they are patient. Heat, after all, works slowly.

## Pyrrhos — The God of Infrared

*Root: πυρρός (pyrrhos) — flame-colored, tawny-red*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Pyrrhos |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Ember Sovereign |
| **Latin Title** | Ignis Occultus — the Hidden Fire |
| **Street Name** | Old Ember |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -THM- |
| **Temple** | The Emberfall Sanctum |

Other epithets: the Red Beneath, the Warmth in the Dark, the First Fire, the Hearth-Father.

Familiar. Grandfatherly. Fading.

Pyrrhos is the oldest of the Seven, or at least the one who feels oldest, the one whose light has dimmed the most, the one whose worshippers have dwindled to a handful of elderly women who leave offerings of coal and cinnamon at his shrine in the Emberfall Sanctum. He was the first fire, the warmth that kept the earliest humans alive through the long dark of prehistory, the glow that meant safety and sustenance and the promise that morning would come. He is the heat that you feel on your skin before you see the flame.

He is fading now. Not dying, for gods do not die easily, but *dimming*, his influence shrinking as the city grows more reliant on the brighter, harsher lights of the visible spectrum. The Body does not bother to bind him further; he is already so thoroughly woven into the fabric of the city that escape is impossible. He is the warmth in the stones of the oldest buildings, the residual heat in the dead embers of a cold forge, the memory of a time when the world was simpler and the gods were kind. The oldest of the old remember him. They leave their offerings. They whisper his true name syllable, -THM-, like a lullaby. And they wait for him to either fade entirely or remember what it means to burn.

## Phaos — The God of Visible Light

*Roots: φάος (phaos) — light*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Phaos |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Prism Sovereign |
| **Latin Title** | Lux Prima — the First Light |
| **Street Name** | The Lamplighter |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -AUR- |
| **Temple** | The Prism Palace |

Other epithets: the Bright Lord, the Revealer, the Painted King, the Lord of Seven Faces, the Clear One.

The working-class god. The one who lit the city. The Body turned him into a municipal employee. He submits willingly.

Phaos is the only god who does not fight his imprisonment, and this is the greatest mystery of the pantheon. He is the spectrum that human eyes can perceive, the colors that paint the world in all its impossible variety. He is the Prism Sovereign, the deity who takes the raw, undivided light of the Unrefracted and splits it into its component parts — red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet — each one a facet of his divine nature. He is the god of art, of sight, of truth, of the beauty that exists in the space between darkness and blinding radiance.

And he is content. The Body bound him into the streetlamps and the prisms of the Prism Palace, and he *allows* it. He does not rage. He does not scheme. He does not whisper rebellion to his cultists. He simply... shines. The Lamplighter, the common folk call him, and there is affection in their voices. He is the god who makes the city beautiful, the god who paints the smog of Lumenspire in shades of amber and rose and gold, the god who ensures that even in the darkest slums, there is always a light to guide you home.

The other gods distrust him. They suspect that his submission is a lie, that he is playing a longer game than any of them can comprehend. The Tuned whisper that Phaos has seen something in the light that the others cannot perceive, that he knows a truth about the Unrefracted that would shatter the pantheon if spoken aloud. But Phaos does not speak. He shines, and he waits, and he smiles, and no one knows what he is thinking.

## Hyperiodes — The God of Ultraviolet

*Root: ὑπεριώδης (hyperiodes) — ultraviolet*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Hyperiodes |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Violet Judge |
| **Latin Title** | Puritas Ardens — Burning Purity |
| **Street Name** | The Beyond-Blue |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -ION- |
| **Temple** | The Sanitarium of Violet |

Other epithets: the Pale Inquisitor, the Stainless Flame, the Unseen Magistrate, the Purifying Ray.

He is the harsh counterpart to Pyrrhos. They are twin flames, though the latter is cruel and therefore feared.

Where Pyrrhos is warmth, Hyperiodes is *burning*. Where Pyrrhos is the glow that comforts, Hyperiodes is the light that *cleanses* with the terrible efficiency of a surgeon's scalpel. He is the radiation that sterilizes and purifies and kills the unseen things that fester in the dark. He is the judge who sees the stains on your soul, the inquisitor who flays away your lies, the flame that burns away everything impure until only the essential remains.

The street calls him The Beyond-Blue, a name that captures both his color and his otherness. He is not like the other gods. He does not seek worship, does not offer comfort, does not promise salvation. He offers only truth — the terrible, purifying truth that burns away all pretense. The Sanitarium of Violet, his temple, is not a place of refuge but a place of trial, where the sick and the guilty and the desperate come to be judged. Most do not survive the experience. Those who do are never the same.

His cultists, the Puritans of Violet, believe that the only way to restore the gods to their former glory is to cleanse the city — to burn away the corruption, the sin, the weakness that has infected Lumenspire since the Fall. They are not popular. They are not meant to be. They are the knife that cuts out the rot, and they know that they will never be loved. Hyperiodes does not ask for love. He asks for purity. And he will burn the world to achieve it, if he must.

## Osteon — The God of X-Rays

*Root: ὀστέον (osteon) — bone*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Osteon |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Bone Seer |
| **Latin Title** | Veritas Penetrans — Piercing Truth |
| **Street Name** | The Bone-Reader |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -SKN- |
| **Temple** | The Ossuary Cathedral |

Other epithets: the Skeleton Judge, the One Who Sees Through, the Pale Cartographer, the Unrobed Truth, the Marrow Prophet.

Fortune-teller. Folk-magic. The one the old women consult. He sees the truth, he tells it, and nobody believes him. Thus, he came to be the patron god of the Seers.

Osteon is the god of X-rays, the radiation that penetrates the flesh and reveals the bones beneath. He is the deity of truth, not the purifying truth of Hyperiodes but the *clinical* truth, the truth that lays bare the skeleton of reality and leaves nothing hidden. He is the god who sees through lies, through secrets, through the careful fictions that people construct to protect themselves from their own natures. He is the Bone Seer, the Marrow Prophet, the one who can look at you and see not the flesh but the framework, not the mask but the skull beneath.

He is also, in the eyes of the common folk, a fortune-teller. This is a corruption of his divine nature, a misunderstanding so profound it would be funny if it were not so tragic. Osteon does not predict the future; he *sees* the present, in all its terrible clarity. But the people of Lumenspire, with their enduring love of superstition and their deep suspicion of actual truth, have reduced him to a carnival trick, a party game, a thing you consult when you want to know if your husband is cheating or your neighbor is stealing your chickens. The old women leave offerings of bones and silver at his shrine, and they ask their petty questions, and Osteon sees the truth and tells it, and no one believes him.

His cultists, the Seers, are not fortune-tellers. They are scholars, philosophers, seekers of the actual truth that Osteon represents. They believe that if they can learn to see the way he sees, they will be able to understand the true nature of the gods, the Fall, and the Unrefracted. They are considered heretics by the Body and lunatics by the street. They do not care. They have seen the truth. And the truth, as Osteon knows, is not something you can unsee.

## Phtheiro — The God of Gamma Rays

*Roots: φθείρω (phtheiro) — to destroy, corrupt, ruin*

| Register | Name |
|---|---|
| **Greek Root-Name** | Phtheiro |
| **Fancy Epithet** | The Radiative Ravager |
| **Latin Title** | Ultima Lux — the Last Light |
| **Street Name** | The Unmaker |
| **True-Name Syllable** | -DTH- |
| **Temple** | The Deadlight |

Other epithets: the Branding Flame, the Last Light, the Cauterizer, the Silence at the End of the Spectrum, the Final Color.

His names say enough about him. If given control, he will unmake everything.

Phtheiro is the most energetic and destructive form of light in the spectrum. He is the end of the line, the final frequency, the light that does not illuminate but *annihilates*. He is the god of decay, of destruction, of the slow rot that eats away at all things until nothing remains but silence and dust. He is the Unmaker, the Radiative Ravager, the Last Light — not the last light you see before death, but the light that *causes* death, the radiation that tears apart cells and unravels DNA and leaves only ruin in its wake.

The orthodox church denies him. They worship Kausterion, the Cauterizer — the aspect of gamma radiation that burns away infection, that sterilizes wounds, that prevents the spread of disease. They acknowledge the destructive power of the final band but insist that it can be harnessed for good, that it is a tool rather than a god, a force rather than a being. This is a comfortable lie, and everyone knows it. The undercity worships Phtheiro openly, in the shadow of the Deadlight, and they do not pretend that he is anything other than what he is.

His cultists, the Unmade, believe that destruction is the only path to renewal, that the gods must be utterly annihilated before they can be reborn. They are the most feared of the undercity cults, not because they are violent — though they are — but because they are *patient*. They do not seek to overthrow the Body, or restore the pantheon, or achieve any of the petty goals of the other sects. They seek the end of everything. And they believe that Phtheiro, when he finally awakens, will grant them their wish.

## Collective Titles

- **The Seven Bands** — street, the most common term, neutral and descriptive
- **The Chromatic Court** — formal, political, used in diplomatic correspondence and legal documents
- **The Refracted Ones** — theological, the fall from wholeness, used by scholars and heretics
- **The Luminous Synod** — church, the official term used by the orthodox clergy
- **The Colorless Throne** — what they sit upon, the vacant seat of the Unrefracted, empty since the First Refraction
- **The First Refraction** — the act that created them, the original sin of the gods
- **The Harrowed** — Seer word, not used by anyone else, a term that acknowledges their suffering and their danger

## The Worshipped and the Denied

Each god has an orthodox aspect and a denied aspect. The Body prefers the orthodox. The undercity prefers the denied.

- **Phonos** is worshipped as the god of communication, the patron of messengers and diplomats. His denied aspect is the god of secrets, the listener who hears everything and tells no one.
- **Thermos** is worshipped as the god of domestic comfort, the patron of cooks and hearth-keepers. His denied aspect is the god of uncontrollable heat, the fire that consumes everything it touches.
- **Pyrrhos** is worshipped as the god of warmth and light, the benevolent grandfather of the pantheon. His denied aspect is the god of the dying fire, the cold that creeps in when the last ember goes out.
- **Phaos** is worshipped as the god of art and beauty, the patron of painters and poets. His denied aspect is the god of blinding light, the radiance that sears the eyes and leaves only darkness behind.
- **Hyperiodes** is worshipped as the god of medicine and hygiene, the patron of doctors and healers. His denied aspect is the god of sterilization, the flame that burns away everything — not just the infection, but the patient as well.
- **Osteon** is worshipped as the god of truth and justice, the patron of judges and scholars. His denied aspect is the god of the skeleton, the truth that lies beneath the flesh, the death that waits in every living body.
- **Phtheiro** is worshipped as the god of decay and renewal, the patron of gravediggers and composters. His denied aspect is the god of annihilation, the end of all things, the silence after the last light dies.

## Heretical Additions

### The White

The hypothetical eighth god. Sum of all light. The one whose coming would unmake the spectrum — or reunite it, depending on who is asked. The White is not a god of any band; it is the *absence of bands*, the undivided light that existed before the First Refraction. Some say it is the Unrefracted itself, waiting to be reborn. Others say it is a new thing, a god that has never existed before, a being that will consume the Seven and replace them with something terrible and beautiful. The Body denies its existence. The Tuned fear it. The Unmade worship it. And somewhere in the Prism Palace, Phaos smiles and says nothing.

### The Blind One

No band. No color. Absolute absence. The anti-god. It sponsors the Body. It wants the extermination of the Seven and the awakening of certain dark things. It fears the protagonist. The Blind One is not a god of darkness — darkness is simply the absence of light, a neutral thing, a natural part of the cycle. The Blind One is the *enemy* of light, the force that seeks to extinguish all radiance, all warmth, all signal, all color. It is the void that existed before the First Refraction, the silence that existed before the first word, the cold that existed before the first fire. It is patient, hungry, and has been waiting for a very, very long time.

### The Unrefracted

The primordial source, before the gods split. The truth the Body is covering up. The thing the Chromaticity was before it became the Chromaticity. The Unrefracted is not a god in the traditional sense; it is the *source* of gods, the ocean from which the Seven were drawn, the light that was shattered into seven distinct frequencies by an act of will that no one fully understands. Some say the Unrefracted chose to split, that it sacrificed its unity to create the diversity of the spectrum. Others say it was murdered, torn apart by its own children in a bid for power. The truth is buried in the archives of the Ossuary Cathedral, in texts that only the Seers can read, in a language that predates the gods themselves. And the Body will do anything to keep it buried.
